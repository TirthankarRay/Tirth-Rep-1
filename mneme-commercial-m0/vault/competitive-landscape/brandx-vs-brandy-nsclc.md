# BrandX vs BrandY — MET-exon14 NSCLC

## Positioning vs Competitor

BrandY (CompetitorCo) was first to market in MET-exon14 NSCLC with its 2023 accelerated
approval and 2025 full approval. It has established prescriber familiarity and
favorable formulary placement at most large commercial plans. BrandX enters the
category second but with several differentiated characteristics that the field team
should reinforce only via MLR-approved messaging.

The most consequential delta is durability: in the registrational program, BrandX
showed a median PFS that runs longer than the BrandY label-reported figure in a
similarly enriched population. Cross-trial comparisons are off-label for promotion,
so all field discussion must rely on the approved single-arm narrative; the longer
PFS may be referenced internally for planning but never in unmodified collateral.

## Clinical Profile Comparison

| Attribute              | BrandX                                  | BrandY                                  |
|------------------------|-----------------------------------------|-----------------------------------------|
| Target                 | MET-exon14                              | MET-exon14                              |
| Modality               | Small molecule, oral                    | Small molecule, oral                    |
| Median PFS (label)     | TBD pending final label                 | 11.0 months (BrandY label, 2025)        |
| ORR (label)            | TBD pending final label                 | 49% (BrandY label, 2025)                |
| Notable AE profile     | Peripheral edema, transaminase elevation| Peripheral edema, fatigue, dyspnea      |
| Dosing                 | Once daily, fixed dose                  | Twice daily                             |

## Commercial Implications

1. **Dosing convenience** is a real promotable advantage; once-daily dosing has
   well-documented adherence benefits in oral oncology.
2. **Tolerability narrative** must be built carefully — both molecules have edema
   liabilities. The pivotal program's lower discontinuation rate is the right framing.
3. **Switch dynamics**: payers will not see BrandX as a step-edit before BrandY;
   parity coverage is realistic and is the market-access ask.
